# Privacy

Pulse has no account system, server, analytics, advertising, crash reporting, or tracking SDKs. It does not collect, sell, or share personal data.

Your exercise library, weekly programme, and settings are stored locally on your device. Today's completion state is kept in a separate date-scoped device cache and is not included in exports or automatic backups. Android system cloud backup is disabled for Pulse. Your data leaves Pulse only when you explicitly export or share it, or when you enable automatic backup to a folder you select through Android's system folder picker.

Online exercise images are disabled by default on new installations. If you enable them, Pulse connects to the internet only when an exercise image is shown. The image is requested directly from the URL you supplied and cached on your device for later offline viewing. Opening a guide hands its URL to your browser. Those services receive the normal connection information described by their own privacy policies; Pulse has no server and does not receive it.

The image cache keeps at most 40 images. You can clear it from Pulse's settings, and disabling online exercise images clears it automatically. It is also removed when you clear Pulse's app storage. Android therefore lists network access for Pulse, but the app does not perform background syncing or contact an account, analytics, advertising, or tracking service.

Automatic backup gives Pulse access only to the folder you select, not to the rest of your storage. You can revoke that access from Pulse's settings. Delayed backups run while Pulse is active; if Android closes the app, an overdue backup runs after you open Pulse again.

You can remove Pulse's stored data by clearing the app's storage or uninstalling it. Export a JSON backup first if you want to keep your programme.
